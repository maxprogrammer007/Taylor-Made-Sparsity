Abstract

A faster, smarter way
to prune neural networks.

Deep neural networks have achieved strong performance in object detection, but their computational cost limits deployment on resource-constrained edge devices. Structured pruning offers a practical solution by removing entire channels or filters to reduce FLOPs and latency. However, commonly used magnitude-based criteria (e.g., L1-norm) are data-independent and often fail to reflect the true functional contribution of network components, leading to significant accuracy degradation at higher sparsity levels.

We propose Taylor-Made Sparsity, a fast and data-driven structured pruning framework based on a first-order Taylor approximation of the loss function. Our method estimates channel importance using the squared gradient–activation interaction, accumulated over a small calibration subset. This provides an efficient proxy for functional sensitivity that computes global importance scores across all layers of a deep network in approximately 1.5 seconds using standard backpropagation.

Extensive experiments on ResNet-50 (CIFAR-10) and MobileNetV2-SSD (MS COCO 2017) demonstrate that the proposed approach consistently improves the accuracy–efficiency trade-off over magnitude-based baselines, remaining stable at pruning ratios up to 50%.

Core Importance Criterion — Taylor-Made Score
STaylorl,i = 𝔼x∼Dcal [ Σh,w ( ∇A(l)i L(x) · A(l)i(x) )2 ]
The importance of the i-th channel in layer l is the expected squared pixel-wise gradient–activation product, accumulated over a calibration subset. Squaring eliminates gradient sign cancellation and stabilizes estimation in multi-task detection settings.
01

First-Order Taylor Approximation

We formalize pruning as loss-preservation: removing channel i perturbs the loss by δA = −A. A first-order Taylor expansion yields a tractable scalar importance score without requiring computationally prohibitive Hessian computation.

02

Squared Grad-Act Proxy

Rather than using the signed first-order estimate — which cancels across spatial positions — we adopt the squared formulation (∇L ⊙ A)². This stabilizes importance estimation under the noisy, multi-objective gradients of object detection losses.

03

Dependency-Aware Structural Pruning

Taylor scores are routed through a dependency graph that encodes residual connections, concatenation, and multi-scale fusion. Channels are pruned synchronously within structural groups, ensuring valid tensor shapes across the full network.

04

Hardware Alignment Constraints

Depthwise convolutions require channel counts divisible by group size. We enforce round_to=8 alignment and restrict pruning to adjacent pointwise layers. Detection heads are excluded to prevent NMS-level misalignments.

Key Findings

What we learned.

⚠️
Magnitude fails at high sparsity

L1-norm heuristics misidentify structurally small but functionally critical bottleneck channels. At >30% sparsity, this causes catastrophic network collapse that gradient-aware methods avoid.

🔧
Structural constraints are non-negotiable

Accurate importance scores alone are insufficient. Without dependency-aware structural alignment, pruning introduces tensor mismatches that halve inference throughput on real hardware.

🎯
10 calibration samples suffice

Performance remains stable between 10–500 calibration samples (95.52%–95.88% accuracy). Even with 10 samples, global importance scoring completes in under 0.25 seconds.
